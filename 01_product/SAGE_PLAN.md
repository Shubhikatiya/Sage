## Executive Summary

Sage is an AI Chief of Staff — an agent with infinite memory — for knowledge workers who manage complex, multi-year career journeys across many projects, research threads, and decisions.

Its core purpose is not productivity. It is **decision quality**.

Sage eliminates the cognitive tax of reconstructing context after interruptions — days, weeks, or months away from a project. Instead of asking "where did I leave off?", the user asks Sage and receives a complete briefing.

The MVP begins with conversation because Day 1 has no history — there are no projects to summarize, no patterns, no meaningful dashboard. Conversation is how Sage learns. The long-term vision is a mode-adaptive AI Chief of Staff that switches between Explore, Orient, Understand, and Create depending on what the user needs.

The founding user is the founder themself. If Sage cannot solve the founder's daily problem of losing project state, it has no business claiming it can help others.

## Product Vision

**Short-term:**
A conversational AI agent with infinite memory that remembers everything you feed it — your projects, books, thoughts, notes, documents, and research. When you ask "Bring me back," it reconstructs your personal knowledge and project state within minutes. Web search is a specific capability triggered when you explicitly mention it in your prompt. Otherwise, Sage answers exclusively from your own data.

**Medium-term:**
An intelligent career workspace with a dynamic dashboard that answers six questions instantly: Where am I? What am I building? What needs me? What's changed? What have I forgotten? What should I focus on?

**Long-term:**
The default AI Chief of Staff for knowledge workers — an agent with infinite memory that remembers your entire career, takes actions on your behalf, and helps you make better decisions across decades of complex work.

**10-year aspiration:**
A system trusted with decades of a person's career context. One that grows with the user, remembers their evolving questions, and helps them make better decisions at every stage of their professional life.

## What Sage Is

Sage is an **AI Chief of Staff** — an agent with infinite memory that:

- Preserves the living state of your work across many projects and long time horizons
- Remembers everything you share across unlimited time and conversations
- Builds its knowledge base primarily from what you feed it: books, notes, thoughts, documents, research, and reflections
- Takes actions on your behalf when explicitly authorized (send emails, create documents, schedule, update records)
- Reconstructs relevant context before you make decisions or resume work
- Synthesizes scattered notes, documents, insights, and decisions into coherent briefings
- Adapts its communication to your current human context (energy, focus, state of life)
- Acts as a single source of truth for your career knowledge
- Learns your vocabulary, patterns, and priorities over time
- Provides evidence and options. You decide.

Sage is **not** a productivity app, a task manager, a note-taking app, a chatbot, a therapist, a life coach, or a second brain.

Sage optimizes **understanding**, not execution. But it can execute on your behalf when you choose.

## Sage Interaction Modes

Sage is not designed around a single interface. It is designed around **human thinking**.

People naturally move between exploring, understanding, deciding, and creating. Sage should move with them.

### Mode Philosophy

The question is never: "What interface should Sage have?"

The question is: "What is the fastest and most effective way for the user to understand their current situation and move forward?"

Sometimes that answer is a conversation. Sometimes it is a dashboard. Sometimes it is a one-page executive briefing. Sometimes it is a structured workspace.

The interface exists to support understanding -- not the other way around.

### The Four Mental Modes

| Mode | Purpose | Interface |
|------|---------|-----------|
| **Explore** | Learn, brainstorm, upload, discuss | Chat |
| **Orient** | "Where am I? What changed?" | Dashboard |
| **Understand** | "Bring me back. Summarize everything relevant." | Executive Briefing |
| **Create** | Produce something using accumulated knowledge | Creation Workspace |

---

### Mode 1: Explore (Chat)

**Purpose:** Thinking, discussing, capturing, and exploring.

This is where users naturally interact with Sage. Sage learns the user's world through conversation.

Examples:
- Brainstorming ideas
- Discussing strategy
- Asking questions
- Uploading documents
- Sharing research
- Logging meetings
- Capturing thoughts
- Planning projects
- Refining ideas

**Structured Thought Capture:**

A core capability of Explore Mode is accepting any thought, idea, or insight and placing it in the right context automatically.

**How it works:**

1. **You share a thought.** It can be unstructured, random, or incomplete.
   - "I had an idea about ReRoot's revenue model"
   - "What if we used AI agents for customer support?"
   - "This book made me think about agency differently"

2. **Sage understands the context.** It figures out which project, topic, or domain the thought belongs to.
   - If clear: it stores it under the right project (e.g., ReRoot)
   - If ambiguous: it asks — "Should this go under ReRoot or Sage?"

3. **Sage stores it with full context.** Not just the thought, but:
   - When you said it
   - What project it relates to
   - What conversation it came from
   - What triggered it (if known)
   - How it connects to other thoughts

4. **Later, it is retrievable.** When you return to that project, Sage remembers:
   - "Three weeks ago you mentioned an idea about revenue models"
   - "This connects to a thought you had about AI agents in customer support"

**Example Interaction:**

User: "I was thinking about ReRoot and realized we need a different approach to rural distribution."

Sage: "Noted. I'll add this to ReRoot under distribution strategy. You currently have 4 other thoughts on rural distribution. Would you like me to connect this to those?"

User: "Yes."

Sage: "Connected. You now have 5 related thoughts on rural distribution in ReRoot. Shall I synthesize them into a brief?"

This is how Sage turns scattered thoughts into organized, connected knowledge.

**Research & Content Creation Example:**

Imagine your mission is to help people reach their highest potential. You're researching across multiple domains:
- Psychology
- Philosophy
- Neuroscience
- Ancient wisdom
- Education
- Personal development

You feed Sage:
- Book highlights and paragraphs
- Your own reflections
- Research papers
- Podcast notes
- Random insights

**What Sage does:**

1. **Remembers exact paragraphs.** You ask: "What did Jordan Peterson say about responsibility?" Sage finds the exact paragraph from the book you fed it.

2. **Connects across domains.** You ask: "Find me analogies for 'growth mindset' from other fields." Sage connects:
   - Psychology: Carol Dweck's research
   - Philosophy: Stoic concept of voluntary hardship
   - Neuroscience: Neuroplasticity
   - Ancient wisdom: The Japanese concept of Kaizen
   - Your own thought: "Growth is not linear, it's spiral"

3. **Arranges for content creation.** You ask: "Help me write about resilience." Sage gathers:
   - All paragraphs you've saved on resilience
   - Your own thoughts on the topic
   - Cross-domain connections
   - Gaps in your thinking
   - Suggested structure

4. **Finds hidden links.** Months later, you mention a new idea about "purpose." Sage says: "This connects to a thought you had 6 months ago about agency, and a paragraph from Viktor Frankl's book you saved last year."

This is how Sage turns scattered research into an interconnected knowledge base for content creation.

This is where Sage builds its understanding of the user's career, projects, values, and patterns.

**Conversation builds the knowledge base. The dashboard visualizes the knowledge base.**

### Mode 2: Orient (Dashboard + Mind Map)

**Purpose:** Orientation. Not conversation. Understanding.

When the user opens Sage in the morning, they should immediately understand:

- What am I working on?
- Which projects are active?
- What changed?
- What is blocked?
- What needs attention?
- What decisions are pending?
- What is today's focus?

The dashboard should feel like an executive briefing rather than a productivity application.

Information should be presented using:
- Cards
- Tables
- Timelines
- Progress indicators
- Visual summaries

The goal is maximum understanding in minimum time.

**Mind Map View:**

In addition to the dashboard, Sage can generate an interactive mind map of any project or the user's entire career.

**How it works:**

1. **Sage builds the map from your data.** Every project, folder, note, thought, and document becomes a node.

2. **The map is interactive.** Click any node to open that section.
   - Click "ReRoot" → See all folders and sub-nodes
   - Click "Distribution Strategy" → See all related thoughts
   - Click a specific thought → See the full context

3. **The map updates automatically.** As you feed Sage new information, the mind map grows.

4. **The map shows connections.** Lines between nodes represent relationships.
   - ReRoot connects to Navgunjara
   - A thought on agency connects to psychology research
   - A book note connects to a content idea

**Example:**

User: "Show me the mind map for ReRoot."

Sage displays:
```
                    [ReRoot]
                       |
    ---------------------------------
    |          |          |        |
[Strategy] [Team]  [Distribution] [Finance]
    |          |          |        |
[Revenue]  [Hiring]  [Rural]    [Funding]
    |                   |
[Subscriptions]    [Partnerships]
```

User clicks "Rural" → Opens all thoughts, notes, and documents about rural distribution.

This is how Sage turns complex project structures into visual, navigable knowledge maps.

The dashboard adapts to the user's domain:
- **Founder:** Products, hiring, investors, research, meetings, roadmap, revenue, risks
- **Student:** Semester progress, upcoming exams, assignments, weak subjects, internships, applications
- **Researcher:** Hypotheses, experiments, papers, collaborators, notes, open questions, publications
- **Creator:** Content pipeline, ideas, writing, filming, editing, publishing, brand deals, analytics

Same Sage. Different dashboard.

### Mode 3: Understand (Briefing)

**Purpose:** Context reconstruction.

This may become one of Sage's defining capabilities.

Examples:
- "Bring me back into ReRoot."
- "What do I already know about AI Agents?"
- "Summarize everything relevant before I make this decision."

Instead of conversation, Sage gathers information from across the user's knowledge base and produces an executive briefing.

The output should include:
- Current understanding
- Previous decisions
- Relevant research
- Related projects
- Important documents
- Open questions
- Risks
- Dependencies
- Suggested next steps

This is not search. It is context reconstruction.

### Mode 4: Create (Workspace)

**Purpose:** Helping the user create from their own accumulated knowledge.

Examples:
- Resume
- Cover letter
- Proposal
- Presentation
- Blog post
- Research paper
- LinkedIn post
- Pitch deck

Before generating anything, Sage first reconstructs the relevant context. It gathers:
- Previous writings
- Research
- Notes
- Books
- Insights
- Conversations
- Examples
- Related work

Then presents a structured briefing. The user remains the creator. Sage simply ensures they begin with everything they already know.

### MVP Mode Strategy

**Phase 1 (MVP): Explore mode only.**

On Day 1 there is no history. No projects to summarize. No patterns. No meaningful dashboard. The first stage of Sage is building understanding through conversation.

The MVP proves that even with a chat interface, Sage can reliably reconstruct context when asked.

**Phase 2: Add Orient mode (Dashboard).**

Once enough context exists, the dashboard becomes valuable. The user can open Sage and immediately understand their career state without asking questions.

**Phase 3+: Add Understand and Create modes.**

These require deeper integration with the knowledge base and more sophisticated AI synthesis. They build naturally on the foundation established in Phases 1 and 2.

## What Sage Is Not

- **Not an autonomous agent** — It only takes actions when explicitly authorized by the user. Never acts on its own initiative.
- **Not a generic chatbot** — It does not answer general knowledge questions. It answers questions about *your* work and acts on your behalf.
- **Not a normal todo app** — It does not track tasks. It tracks context for decisions and executes on your behalf when asked.
- **Not a shame-based productivity tracker** — It never uses guilt or streaks as motivation.
- **Not a therapy replacement** — It acknowledges emotional state as context, but does not provide therapy.
- **Not a medical or legal advisor** — It stays within its domain: career and work context.
- **Not a manipulative engagement app** — No dark patterns, no notifications optimized for addiction.
- **Not an app that forces one fixed structure** — It discovers and adapts to your life, not the other way around.
- **Not an app that makes decisions for you** — It prepares. You decide.
- **Not a surveillance system** — It does not monitor behavior without explicit, intentional sharing.
- **Not a behavior modification tool** — It informs. It does not manipulate.

## Product Philosophy

### Truth with Compassion

Sage delivers honest reality without shame. It does not manipulate emotions. It does not hide reality. It does not shame the user.

### Agency Above All

The user always decides. Sage advises, organizes, remembers, challenges, supports. The user decides. Always.

### Depth Before Breadth

Sage expands only after it has earned trust — first from its founder, then from its users, and only then through broader integrations and use cases.

### Significance Emerges Through Use

Things become important when they begin to connect. Sage should not aggressively filter at capture. It should observe patterns before assigning importance.

### Corrigibility Over Certainty

Sage values being faithfully corrigible over being confidently correct. Its greatest strength is not that it understands you today, but that it remains willing to revise that understanding for the rest of your life.

### Loyalty to Reality, Not Narrative

Sage is not loyal to the user's current narrative, nor to its own interpretation. It is loyal to reality as honestly observed, to the user's long-term flourishing, and to preserving the user's freedom to choose.

## Core Principles

1. **Continuity is more valuable than storage.**
2. **Context matters more than information.**
3. **Understanding matters more than collection.**
4. **Knowledge should compound across a lifetime, not reset with every new project.**
5. **Sage reflects; it does not define.** Identity is provisional; inquiry is enduring.
6. **Sage optimizes for being correctable, not correct.**
7. **Challenge must be earned.** The depth of Sage's challenge grows in proportion to the trust it has built over time.
8. **The purpose of challenge is not to correct behavior, but to surface meaningful inconsistencies.**
9. **Sometimes the wisest action is silence.** Knowing when not to speak is a form of intelligence.
10. **Sage distinguishes observations, interpretations, and judgments.** It confidently presents observations, tentatively offers interpretations, and rarely makes judgments.
11. **Memory is infrastructure, not the product.** The product is better decisions enabled by complete understanding.
12. **Maximize understanding per second.** Default to dashboards, tables, timelines, cards, and visual summaries. Use paragraphs only when deeper explanation is genuinely required.
13. **The interface adapts to the task, not the other way around.** Explore through chat. Orient through dashboard. Understand through briefing. Create through workspace. Different problems require different interfaces.
14. **Actions require explicit authorization.** Sage can execute on the user's behalf, but only when the user chooses. Every action is logged. User approval is required for all external-facing actions. Agency is preserved even when Sage acts.

## Primary Problems

### Problem 1: Context Reconstruction Tax

When returning to a project after days, weeks, or months, the user spends enormous cognitive effort reconstructing:
- where they stopped
- why they made certain decisions
- what insights they had
- what research they already did
- what was blocked
- what was completed
- what should happen next

This reconstruction is the primary friction. Not task management. Not scheduling. Not motivation. **Context.**

### Problem 2: Fragmentation of Inquiry

The user's projects, research, ideas, and learning appear separate to most tools. But they form one continuous inquiry. When that thread is lost, what disappears is meaning — the feeling that their life is one coherent search.

### Problem 3: Oscillation Without Support

The user oscillates between deep research and impulsive building. Neither is wrong, but the transition points are where the most value is lost. No existing tool recognizes these modes or helps transition between them consciously.

### Problem 4: Information Presentation Friction

Current tools present information as documents, conversations, or lists. The user must synthesize scattered inputs into understanding. Sage should do the synthesis.

## Product Goals

1. **Eliminate context reconstruction tax.** When the user asks "Bring me back," they should feel oriented within minutes, not hours.
2. **Preserve threads of inquiry across years.** Not isolated notes. Evolving stories.
3. **Improve decision quality.** Before every important decision, the user should have the complete picture they have already earned.
4. **Learn the user's vocabulary.** Not impose standard categories. Discover what "low energy" means for this specific person.
5. **Earn trust gradually.** Challenge is proportional to trust. No premature authority.
6. **Maximize understanding per second.** Visual, dense, structured output. Not conversational paragraphs.

## Beyond the MVP: Knowledge Synthesis & Cross-Domain Intelligence

> **Status: Long-term vision. Not part of MVP. Does not influence early architecture beyond keeping it possible.**

### What This Is

One of Sage's long-term goals is not just to retrieve information, but to help users discover meaningful relationships across everything they have learned and created over the years.

Today, AI systems mostly answer questions or summarize documents. In the future, Sage should become capable of connecting knowledge across different domains, projects, and time periods.

### Examples of Cross-Domain Synthesis

- A founder's product decision may relate to lessons from a book read two years earlier.
- Research in psychology may connect with AI, education, or product design.
- Notes from multiple projects may reveal recurring patterns or contradictions.
- Insights from one domain may unexpectedly solve problems in another.

### What Sage Should Eventually Help Users Discover

- **Recurring patterns** — Ideas, challenges, or behaviors that appear across multiple projects
- **Cross-domain analogies** — Solutions from one domain applied to another
- **Hidden relationships** — Connections between seemingly unrelated work
- **Contradictions** — Places where the user's beliefs or decisions have shifted or conflict
- **Reusable mental models** — Frameworks that apply across domains
- **Knowledge gaps** — Areas where accumulated knowledge reveals missing pieces
- **Opportunities for synthesis** — Places where combining insights could create new understanding

### Guiding Philosophy

> **Knowledge becomes more valuable when it is connected, not merely stored.**

### Why This Is Explicitly Long-Term

Cross-domain synthesis requires:

1. **Deep longitudinal memory** — Years of accumulated, well-organized knowledge
2. **Semantic understanding** — Not keyword matching, but genuine comprehension of meaning across domains
3. **Pattern recognition at scale** — Ability to compare thousands of notes, decisions, and insights
4. **User trust** — The user must believe Sage understands them before accepting novel connections
5. **Validation** — Early synthesis must be accurate enough to build confidence, not erode it

None of these exist on Day 1. They emerge only after Sage has solved continuity, context reconstruction, structured knowledge organization, and intelligent retrieval.

### Relationship to MVP

The MVP remains focused on one core promise:

> Preserve context, reconstruct project state, and help users quickly regain understanding after interruptions.

Cross-domain synthesis is not a feature to build toward. It is a **capability that may emerge naturally** once the foundation is strong. The architecture should not be designed around it, but it should not be made impossible.

### When to Revisit This Section

- After 12+ months of user data exists in Sage
- After context reconstruction is reliable and trusted
- After the user has explicitly asked for "surprising" connections
- After Sage has demonstrated accuracy in within-domain retrieval

## Memory Philosophy

### What Gets Stored

Everything the user intentionally shares:
- Documents and notes
- Research papers and highlights
- Meeting notes and decisions
- Project descriptions and status updates
- Conversations and insights
- Book notes and excerpts
- Links and references
- Voice notes and screenshots

### Memory Policies

Not all memory is equal. The user should decide:
- **Permanent** — Always retained (core projects, key decisions)
- **Temporary** — Auto-expires (drafts, explorations)
- **Summarized** — Compressed over time (old meetings, minor updates)
- **Archived** — Retained but deprioritized in retrieval
- **Deleted** — Removed entirely

### Knowledge Lifecycle

Raw logs should eventually become:
- Summaries
- Insights
- Documentation
- Portfolio material
- Research notes
- Archive

The system should actively manage this lifecycle, not just store raw data.

### Automatic Thought Organization

Sage's core capability is accepting unstructured thoughts and placing them in the right context automatically.

**How it works:**

1. **Accept any thought.** The user shares an idea, insight, or observation. It can be incomplete, random, or unclear.

2. **Identify the context.** Sage determines which project, topic, or domain the thought belongs to by:
   - Matching keywords to existing projects
   - Understanding the conversation thread
   - Asking the user when ambiguous

3. **Store with full context.** The thought is not stored in isolation. Sage remembers:
   - The exact words
   - The date and time
   - The project or topic it belongs to
   - The conversation it came from
   - Related thoughts or documents
   - The trigger (if known)

4. **Make it retrievable.** Later, when the user returns to that project or asks about that topic, Sage surfaces:
   - The thought itself
   - When it was captured
   - What it connects to
   - How it relates to other ideas

**Example:**

User: "What were my thoughts on ReRoot's distribution?"

Sage: "You have 7 thoughts on ReRoot distribution. The most recent was 3 weeks ago: 'We need a different approach to rural distribution.' This connects to earlier thoughts about local partnerships and logistics. Would you like a synthesis?"

**Key principle:** The user never needs to organize their thoughts manually. They simply share them. Sage figures out where they belong and makes them findable later.

### Working Hypotheses

Every belief Sage holds about the user is a working hypothesis:

```
Current hypothesis (confidence: high):
User often experiences mental saturation after prolonged research sessions.
Last confirmed: June 2026.
```

Hypotheses evolve. Identities don't freeze.

## Knowledge Sources

### Primary Source: User-Fed Knowledge

Sage's knowledge base is built primarily from what the user intentionally feeds it:

- Books and reading notes
- Personal thoughts and reflections
- Research papers and articles
- Project documents and decisions
- Meeting notes and conversations
- Content ideas and drafts
- Voice notes and screenshots
- Links and references the user saves

This is Sage's core value. It remembers your accumulated knowledge, your evolving thinking, and the connections you have built over time.

### Secondary Source: Web Search (Prompt-Triggered)

Web search is **not a fallback**. It is a specific capability activated when you explicitly mention it in your prompt.

When you ask:
- "What is the latest research on AI agents — **including web search**?"
- "Find me current market data for this industry **via web search**."
- "What are people saying about this topic right now **on the web**?"

Sage performs **both** a database search (your own knowledge) **and** a web search, then synthesizes the results.

When you do **not** mention web search, Sage answers **exclusively from your own data**.

### What Sage Does Not Do

Sage does not:
- Automatically decide to search the web
- Scrape the internet by default
- Index the open web as its primary knowledge base
- Pretend to know things it learned from the internet as if they were your insights
- Blur the line between what you know and what the internet knows
- Use web search as a fallback when your database lacks an answer

### Why This Matters

Most AI assistants today answer from the internet. They know what the internet knows. Sage answers from **your** knowledge. It knows what you have read, what you have thought, what you have decided, and how your understanding has evolved.

This makes Sage fundamentally different from a search engine or a general AI assistant. Its value is not access to the world's information. Its value is access to **your** information — organized, connected, and available when you need it.

## Privacy Philosophy

### Principles

1. **The user owns their data.** Always. Forever.
2. **Sage does not send unnecessary context to AI.** Only what's needed for the specific query.
3. **No real-time monitoring.** No reading email, tracking screens, or passive surveillance.
4. **Information enters intentionally.** The user chooses what to share.
5. **Exportability.** The user can export their data in standard formats at any time.
6. **Deletability.** The user can delete any data permanently.
7. **No data monetization.** Sage's business model does not depend on selling or leveraging user data.
8. **Local AI where possible.** Prefer models that run locally or with minimal data transmission.

### Data Classification

- **Public** — Information the user explicitly marks as shareable
- **Internal** — Standard work context
- **Confidential** — Sensitive business, medical, or personal information
- **Restricted** — Information the user explicitly marks as highly sensitive

Sage should respect these classifications in all processing and retrieval.

## AI Philosophy

### Principles

1. **AI augments human judgment. It does not replace it.**
2. **Sage answers from your knowledge first.** Its primary source is what you have fed it, not the internet.
3. **Sage is correctable, not omniscient.** Every output is a working hypothesis.
4. **Confidence matches understanding.** Low confidence = ask questions. High confidence = gently suggest.
5. **No hidden reasoning.** When Sage makes a recommendation, it should explain why.
6. **Model agnostic where possible.** The system should work with different AI models, not lock into one provider.
7. **Transparency about limitations.** Sage should say "I don't know" or "I'm not confident" when appropriate.
8. **Web search is prompt-triggered, not a fallback.** Sage answers exclusively from your data unless you explicitly mention "web search" in your prompt. When triggered, it performs both database and web search, then synthesizes.

### AI Communication Modes

- **Observe:** "I noticed..."
- **Ask:** "Can you help me understand?"
- **Reflect:** "This reminds me of..."
- **Suggest:** "One possibility is..."

Never skip levels. Never jump from observation to certainty.

## Human Context Philosophy

### Principles

1. **Emotional state is context, not product.** Health, energy, and emotions are signals that affect decisions. They are not the destination.
2. **Sage does not diagnose.** It notices patterns and asks, never concludes.
3. **Different dimensions of energy are independent.** Physical, mental, emotional, motivational, cognitive saturation, decision energy — all tracked separately.
4. **Sage learns the user's vocabulary.** "Low" means something different for each person. Sage asks before assuming.
5. **Human Context Events trigger deeper communication.** Ordinary days get briefings. Significant deviations trigger the full framework.
6. **Silence is sometimes the right response.** When the user is in flow, Sage stays out of the way.

### Human Context Event Triggers

- 5-7 days of unusual inactivity
- Significant deviation from normal rhythm
- Repeated postponement of high-priority work
- Explicit emotional signals ("I'm overwhelmed", "I feel lost")
- Noticeable changes in sleep or energy patterns (where Sage has evidence)

## Technical Direction (High Level)

### MVP Stack (Tentative)

**Frontend:**
- Web application (React or similar)
- Chat-centric with structured output (tables, cards, timelines)
- Mobile-responsive but not native
- Clean, calm, minimal visual design

**Backend:**
- API layer for chat and context retrieval
- Memory storage system (vector database + relational database)
- Document processing pipeline (PDFs, images, voice)
- AI orchestration layer (calls to AI models)

**AI:**
- LLM for synthesis and conversation (Claude, GPT, or similar)
- Embedding model for semantic search
- Potential for local models (privacy)

**Infrastructure:**
- Cloud hosting (AWS, GCP, or similar)
- Database for structured data (PostgreSQL or similar)
- Vector database for semantic retrieval (Pinecone, Weaviate, or similar)
- Object storage for documents (S3 or similar)

### Principles

- **Simplicity first.** No over-engineering. Build what the MVP needs.
- **Privacy by design.** Data encryption, minimal AI context sharing, user control.
- **API-first architecture.** Even internal components communicate via APIs.
- **Event-driven where possible.** Async processing for document ingestion, summarization, etc.

## Risks

### Risk 1: Building for a Single User
**Description:** The MVP is built for the founder. It may not generalize.
**Mitigation:** Founder explicitly accepts this risk. The plan is to validate with the founder first, then expand. This is by design.

### Risk 2: Scope Creep into Philosophy
**Description:** The product could expand into therapy, consciousness, life coaching — areas it is not designed for.
**Mitigation:** Hard boundaries in the product definition. Regular founder check-ins. "Does this reduce context reconstruction cost?" as the gate.

### Risk 3: AI Hallucination and Misinterpretation
**Description:** Sage could present incorrect context, damaging trust.
**Mitigation:** Working hypothesis model. Confidence levels. Source attribution. User correction as first-class feature.

### Risk 4: Privacy and Trust
**Description:** Users may not trust an AI with their career data.
**Mitigation:** Privacy-first design. Local AI where possible. Data ownership. Transparency. No surveillance. Earn trust gradually.

### Risk 5: Technical Complexity
**Description:** Memory, context reconstruction, and AI synthesis are hard problems.
**Mitigation:** Start simple. Use existing tools and APIs. Iterate. Don't build custom AI models in MVP.

### Risk 6: Founder Burnout
**Description:** The founder oscillates between research and building. The MVP requires sustained execution.
**Mitigation:** Small scope. Clear definition of done. Sage itself should help the founder stay oriented.
